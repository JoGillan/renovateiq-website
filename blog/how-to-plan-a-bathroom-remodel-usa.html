How to Plan a Bathroom Remodel — The Complete US Guide

By RenovateIQ · 11 min read · Bathroom Remodel

A bathroom remodel is one of the most impactful home improvements you can make. Buyers notice bathrooms immediately — a well-executed remodel adds real value to your home and transforms your daily routine. But bathrooms are also one of the most complex rooms to remodel, with plumbing, electrical, waterproofing and tile work all in a small space.

This guide walks you through everything you need to know to plan your bathroom remodel properly — from setting a budget to finding a contractor and managing the project from start to finish.

How much does a bathroom remodel cost in the US?

Bathroom remodel costs vary significantly by location, scope and the fixtures you choose. Labor costs in cities like New York, San Francisco and Boston run much higher than the national average. Here are typical US cost ranges:

ProjectBudgetMid-RangeHigh-End
Full bathroom remodel$5,000–$15,000$15,000–$30,000$30,000–$75,000
Master bathroom remodel$10,000–$20,000$20,000–$40,000$40,000–$100,000

Cosmetic refresh only$1,500–$5,000$5,000–$12,000$12,000–$25,000

Always add a 15-20% contingency — hidden plumbing issues and water damage discovered during demo are common in bathroom remodels.

Step 1: Define your scope

Before spending anything, decide what kind of remodel you're doing:

  • Full gut remodel: Everything comes out — fixtures, tile, drywall, sometimes subfloor. Maximum cost but maximum transformation.
  • Partial remodel: New fixtures but keeping existing tile, or new tile but keeping fixtures. Good middle ground on cost.
  • Cosmetic refresh: New vanity, lighting, hardware and paint. Minimal disruption, surprisingly big impact.

Moving plumbing — relocating the toilet, shower or tub — significantly increases cost. Working with existing drain locations saves money and time.

💡 Pro tip

Before writing your brief, spend a week noting every frustration with your current bathroom. Not enough storage? Weak shower pressure? Poor lighting? Your remodel should fix real problems, not just make things look nicer.

Step 2: Check permits

Most bathroom remodels require permits, particularly for plumbing and electrical work. Permit requirements vary significantly by city and county. Always check with your local building department before starting work.

Never skip permits to save time or money. Unpermitted work can cause major problems when you sell your home, may not be covered by your homeowner's insurance and could require you to undo completed work. Your contractor should pull permits — always confirm this upfront.

Step 3: Choose your fixtures and tile

Vanity and sink

Floating vanities look contemporary and make cleaning easier but require solid blocking in the wall during framing. Freestanding vanities are simpler to install. Single sink vanities are standard; double sinks are popular in master bathrooms. Quality faucets make a significant visual difference — budget more here than on the vanity itself.

Shower and tub

Walk-in showers without a door are the most in-demand feature in US bathroom remodels right now. Frameless glass enclosures look the most premium. Soaking tubs are desirable in master bathrooms but add significant cost. Tub/shower combos are practical for family bathrooms. Curbless showers require more extensive waterproofing but are fully accessible.

Tile

Large format tiles (24x24 or bigger) are trending and make bathrooms feel more spacious. Porcelain is more durable than ceramic for floors and wet areas. Subway tile remains a classic, versatile choice. Budget 10-15% extra for cuts and wastage. Floor tile must be rated for floor use — don't use wall tile on floors.

Toilet

Wall-hung toilets look sleek and simplify cleaning but require a carrier frame. Standard floor-mounted toilets are simpler and cheaper to install. One-piece toilets are easier to clean than two-piece. Comfort height (chair height) models are increasingly popular and required under ADA for accessible bathrooms.

Step 4: Find your contractor

For a bathroom remodel you'll typically need:

  • A licensed plumber for supply and drain work
  • A licensed electrician for lighting, outlets and exhaust fan
  • A tile setter for floor and wall tile
  • A general contractor or bathroom remodeling specialist to coordinate everything

Always verify that contractors are licensed in your state and carry both general liability insurance and workers' compensation. Get at least three itemized quotes. Check reviews on Houzz, Angi or the Better Business Bureau.

💡 Pro tip

Ask to see photos of recently completed bathroom projects specifically — not just general remodeling work. Tile setting and waterproofing are specialist skills. A contractor who is great at kitchens isn't necessarily great at bathrooms.

Step 5: Timeline

A full bathroom remodel typically takes 2-3 weeks. Here's a realistic breakdown:

  • Day 1-2: Demo, rough plumbing and electrical
  • Day 2-4: Backer board, waterproofing/membrane installation
  • Day 4-8: Tile — floor and walls
  • Day 8-12: Vanity, toilet, shower fixtures, second fix plumbing and electrical
  • Day 12-14: Grouting, caulking, accessories, final inspections, punch list

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Inadequate waterproofing: The leading cause of bathroom failures. Cement board alone is not waterproof — proper membrane installation is essential.
  • Undersized exhaust fan: Size your exhaust fan for the cubic footage of the bathroom, not just the square footage. Poor ventilation causes mold.
  • Choosing tile before finalizing layout: Lock down your layout before ordering tile so you know exactly what you need.
  • Skipping the punch list: Walk the finished bathroom carefully and note every issue before making final payment.
  • Not planning storage: Beautiful bathrooms always have enough storage. Plan built-in niches and medicine cabinets from the start.
