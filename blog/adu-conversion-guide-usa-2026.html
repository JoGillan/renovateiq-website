ADU Conversion Guide 2026 — Costs, Zoning and Planning Basics

By RenovateIQ · 9 min read · Planning

Demand for accessory dwelling units is rising fast in 2026, driven by multigenerational living, aging-in-place planning, and homeowners looking for rental income potential. Whether it's a detached backyard unit, a garage conversion, or a basement suite, ADUs are one of the most flexible ways to add usable space and long-term value to a property.

Here's what to know before you start — costs, zoning basics, and how to budget realistically.

What is an ADU?

An accessory dwelling unit is a self-contained secondary living space on the same lot as a primary home — with its own entrance, kitchen or kitchenette, bathroom, and sleeping area. ADUs can be detached (a separate structure in the backyard), attached (built onto the main house), or converted from existing space like a garage or basement.

Popular ADU types and typical US costs

🏡 Detached new-build ADU
$100,000–$300,000+

A fully independent structure built from scratch in the backyard. Cost varies enormously by region, size, and finish level, but this is a genuine ground-up construction project, not a light renovation — budget and permitting timelines should reflect that.

🚗 Garage conversion ADU
$40,000–$100,000

Often the fastest and most cost-effective route since the structure, foundation and roof already exist. Main costs are insulation, plumbing, electrical upgrades, and bringing the space up to livability code.

🔽 Basement conversion ADU
$60,000–$150,000+

Requires proper egress windows or doors for safety code, waterproofing, and often ceiling height adjustments. A strong option where lot size limits a detached build, but requires a contractor experienced specifically in basement-to-living-space conversions.

Zoning and permitting: what to check first

ADU rules vary dramatically by state, city, and even individual lot — this is the single biggest variable in any ADU project, more than construction cost itself. Before committing to a design:

  • Confirm your lot is zoned for an ADU — many states have relaxed ADU zoning significantly in recent years, but local ordinances still vary widely on size, height, and setback requirements
  • Check owner-occupancy requirements — some jurisdictions require the main house or the ADU to be owner-occupied, which affects rental plans
  • Understand parking requirements — some areas require additional off-street parking for an ADU, which can affect feasibility on a small lot
  • Building code applies regardless of zoning approval — egress, ceiling height, fire separation and utilities all need to meet code independent of the zoning permit
💡 Pro tip

Call your local planning department before hiring a designer, not after. A quick pre-application conversation is far cheaper than redesigning a unit that turns out to violate a setback or height rule you didn't know applied to your lot.

Budgeting for an ADU project

ADU projects run longer than most remodels and involve more sequential phases — permitting, groundwork, structure, utilities, interior fit-out — which means more opportunities for delay and cost creep. Build in a larger contingency than a standard remodel (20–25% rather than 15–20%), and track spend by phase rather than as one lump sum, so you can catch a phase running over before it drags the whole budget with it.
