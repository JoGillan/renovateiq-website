Kitchen Remodel Cost Guide 2026 — The Complete US Breakdown

By RenovateIQ · 9 min read · Kitchen

Kitchens are expected to be the single most popular remodel of 2026, driven by demand for multifunctional layouts, smart appliances, and more elevated finishes. It's the room you use every day, and it consistently returns more value per dollar spent than almost any other project — but "kitchen remodel" covers everything from a cabinet refresh to a full gut renovation, so the price range is enormous.

Here's a realistic breakdown by scope, where the money goes, and where budgets most often blow past their original number.

Typical US costs by scope

🎨 Refresh
$3,000–$8,000

Repaint or reface existing cabinets, new countertop, new hardware. Works well if the layout functions and the cabinet boxes are in good shape. Increasingly popular as homeowners look to update rather than replace — good cabinetry often just needs a facelift, not a rebuild.

🍳 Mid-range remodel
$15,000–$35,000

New cabinets, countertops and appliances, same footprint and plumbing locations. This is what most homeowners picture when they say "kitchen remodel." Keeping the sink and range where they already are keeps costs down significantly — relocating plumbing or gas lines adds cost fast.

🏗️ Full remodel with layout changes
$45,000–$90,000+

Removing walls, adding an island, expanding the footprint. This is where budgets most commonly overrun, because structural surprises — outdated wiring, plumbing that doesn't meet current code, water damage behind walls — tend to surface once demolition starts.

Where the money actually goes

  • Cabinets: 25–35% of total budget
  • Countertops: 10–15% (natural stone and quartz sit at the top of this range)
  • Appliances: 15–20%
  • Labor (installation, plumbing, electrical): 20–35%
  • Flooring and backsplash: 10–15%

What's trending for 2026

Kitchens are shifting away from all-white, high-contrast looks toward warmer, more layered palettes — natural stone slabs, wood tones, and richer colors like olive and clay. Smart appliances (touchless faucets, voice-controlled ovens, smart refrigeration) are becoming standard rather than a luxury upsell. Open-concept layouts remain popular, but with more thoughtful zoning that separates cooking, prep, and entertaining areas instead of one undivided space.

The single biggest reason kitchen budgets overrun

A large share of homeowners who remodeled last year went over their initial budget, and the two most common causes were unexpected costs discovered mid-project and homeowners upgrading their selections partway through — a nicer slab, a better range — once they'd already committed to the disruption. Both are avoidable the same way: build in a 15–20% contingency above your quoted budget before starting, and track every invoice against it as you go instead of reviewing spend only at the end.

💡 Pro tip

Ask every contractor to itemize labor separately from materials on their bid. It makes comparing quotes far easier, and it's a useful early signal of how transparent a contractor will be once the job is underway.

Getting it right

  1. Get at least three bids for any remodel over $10,000
  2. Set your contingency before you start — not after the first surprise
  3. Track spend by category so you can see you're most of the way through your countertop allowance before you commit to the expensive slab, not after
  4. Match your spend to your home's value — an ultra high-end kitchen in a modest home rarely pays itself back at resale
